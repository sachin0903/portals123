Graphene · platform integration review

Morgan Lloyd workflows, reconciled against SEI and the build

Thirteen workflow spreadsheets describe how a client's money moves between an adviser, Graphene, Morgan Lloyd and SEI. This reads each one against the Dynamics operations that would carry it, the SEI endpoints and code sets on the other side, and what the platform has actually built — so a step that nobody owns is visible as a step that nobody owns.

The shape of it

Two workflows run unattended. Two more have their Graphene half built and their provider half missing. The remaining nine are operations procedures with an API sitting next to them unused — and in six of those nine the vendor sheet says so, which is worth more than a guess.

What SEI actually publishes — and what it does not

The reconciliation the sheets ask for assumes SEI has statuses to compare against. Mostly it does not, and knowing which is which decides whether a step can be automated or has to be watched.

There is no transfer entity at SEI. A transfer's SEI leg is an anticipated cash receipt and then a credit in the transaction feed. That is why a transfer completes at our reconciliation and why Morgan Lloyd's last forward state (“Funds sent”) is the furthest the vendor can take it.

There is no read-back for a cash receipt. createCurrencyReceipt is the only endpoint published on the service; a receipt, once created, is reconciled through the transaction feed rather than queried. The activityId it returns is the handle to record, and a 200 can still carry warnings.

There is no lifecycle status on a transaction. A row in the report has settled; ACTUAL_SETTLEMENT_DATE is the only column that says so. Our conformed model publishes status as an explicit NULL for SEI rather than inventing one.

What stands in their place

Why 21121 and 21122 matter more than they look

A contribution and the basic-rate relief that follows it both arrive as type 6 Cash Receipt on the same account. Only the description code separates them — 21121 the member's net contribution, 21122 the relief. Without it the reconciliation counts the relief as a second contribution and double-counts the annual allowance. Both code sets are carried in full by the nightly Reference report; there is no lookup endpoint for them.

Gaps

The SEI half of step 3 has no API behind it. The sheet reads as though both calls are symmetric; they are not. pkgs/sei publishes one service — /currencyreceiptservices — so opening the third-party account is an out-of-band process today, and the sheet's own requirement 2.a (“ascertain if our account open form is in line with the required data from SEI”) is still open.

Four of the six ML steps are unimplemented. Contact and scheme run and are resumable; beneficiaries, transfers, valuation and documents do not.

Two things that bit, worth not relearning

MORGAN_LLOYD_ADVISER_FIRM_ID must be sent as cam_financialadviser_Account and as an OData reference (/accounts(<guid>)), not a bare GUID. Without it the contact is unattached and every later cam_createscheme 403s.

The custom actions answer with the id nested under response, not as a top-level contactid — which is why an earlier version created the contact every time, discarded the id, and duplicated the person on retry.

One open measurement

The model's own annual charge is now sent as the fund's management_charge; before that it was never sent at all. Whether CTC honours it on an accumulation quote is unmeasured — fund-level rate_of_return is overridden there by the COBS standard rates, so an element being accepted is not evidence it is used. One run with and without, on the same scenario, settles it.

Gaps

Step 6 is the only fully manual step in an otherwise automated flow, and it is the one that moves real money. There is no batch identifier on either side to tie a relief credit to the contribution that earned it, which is why relief is reconciled at account grain rather than per contribution.

BACS Direct Debit is not built — it needs SEI Payer Profiles, so a regular contribution is expected rather than collected. The matcher is on-demand; nothing runs it on a timer.

Why completed has no vendor source

Morgan Lloyd's furthest forward state is “Funds sent”: the money has left them for the custodian. Whether it arrived is a fact only the custodian's feed carries, and SEI has no transfer record to ask. So a transfer completes at our reconciliation and nowhere else — which is also why steps 3–5 of the sheet cannot be collapsed into “wait for ML to say done”.

Gaps

Both provider legs are missing. A transfer raised from an account today produces a Graphene record and a forecast that no one has been told about — ML does not know to expect the request, and SEI does not know to expect the money. The sheet's step 4 asks whether the ACR can be automated; it can, and the answer recorded at the time was “not for day one”.

The naming trap in this one

transfer_type already exists on platform.transfers and means something else — the wrapper the money is going into, derived from the account's product. Adding in-specie must not overload it. The vendor keeps them as two separate option sets (TypeOfTransfer and TransferType) and so should we.

One operational improvement the sheet asks for and nobody owns: looking assets up before raising the SEI instruction, so a Free Receipt is not rejected for an asset SEI does not hold. The comment notes the ops procedure omits it.

The step that decides the timing

Step 3 says the disbursement need not wait for ML — but nothing tells Graphene a withdrawal has started, so in practice it does wait. BenefitEventStatus (in progress, live, not taken up, stopped) is the state to poll, and cam_schemememberevents is the call. That single unbuilt poll is what turns a one-day disbursement into a ten-day one.

Scheme fees

Manual

ML Fee Work Flow 3.6.26.xlsx

  1. 1Morgan LloydSends a file of clients with fees due, in the first week of each month, so fees can be paid by the 15th.
  2. 2GrapheneContacts the adviser: a disinvestment is needed.
  3. 3AdviserSays whether to sell or use available cash.

  5. 5SEISends the money to ML.

Nothing here is built, and one answer is already recorded

The sheet asks whether SEI could run an annual fee on the anniversary date. The answer captured in it is no — SEI cannot, and the amount can change year to year. So the monthly file, the adviser conversation and the disbursement are all manual by necessity, not by omission.

What is automatable and unbuilt: ingesting the monthly file, and reconciling step 6 against the fee rows in the feed (types 12–14, 117, 118) rather than confirming by spreadsheet. The catalogue already models fee packages — from Seccl's register, not ML's file.

What is missing before any of this can be tracked

There is no death record, no date-of-death field, and no account freeze. The client record holds a vulnerability block but nothing about death, so the trigger for every branch above is a person remembering. Morgan Lloyd models it — BenefitEventType 7, death benefits — and cam_schemememberevents would surface it, but that call is unused.

Beneficiary nominations are collected at onboarding and never pushed (cam_createbeneficiaries, workflow 01), which is precisely the data step 3 turns on.

A defect in the source sheet

Row 2's requirement column reads “Graphene to send request to CTC via API. CTC to return PDF of illustration via API” — that is the illustration workflow's text, pasted into the death workflow. The step it sits on is about requesting a death certificate and will. Worth correcting at source before anyone builds from it.

Ill health

Manual

ML ill health Work Flow 23.6.26.xlsx — the sheet's own first line: “This process is manual at ML so no automation is available.”

  1. 1AdviserTells us the client is in ill health and wants to take benefits early.
  2. 2GrapheneRequests evidence from the doctor and emails ML that the process has begun.
  3. 3AdviserProvides a letter confirming the client cannot work in their profession again for life.
  4. 4Morgan LloydConfirms eligibility. If eligible, a paper benefit form goes to the adviser.
  5. 5GrapheneShares the returned form with ML, then follows the standard withdrawal flow — without the DCube step.
Why DCube is skipped

There is no standard DCube journey for a member under 55, so the benefit form is paper and the adviser may sign on the client's behalf. Everything after that rejoins workflow 06 at step 3 — the disbursement is identical. The only thing worth building here is the document trail, not the decision.

Serious ill health

Manual

ML serious ill health Work Flow 23.6.26.xlsx — also marked manual at ML

  1. 1AdviserTells us the client has less than twelve months to live and wants to withdraw all funds tax free.
  2. 2GrapheneRequests evidence and notifies ML.
  3. 3AdviserProvides the doctor's letter. The sheet confirms there is no ML form for this — the letter is the evidence.
  4. 4Morgan LloydConfirms eligibility; the standard withdrawal process then applies.

Where it differs from workflow 10

Ill health needs a paper form because there is no DCube journey under 55; serious ill health uses the standard full withdrawal. Morgan Lloyd distinguishes them in its own event taxonomy — BenefitEventType 8 is serious ill health — so if the event poll is ever built, the two are separable without our having to infer which is which.
