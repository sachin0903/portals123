Where a value comes from

An illustration is assembled from four places, and only one of them is the form. The adviser types the assumptions; the client's identity, the fund value and the growth basis are all read from records that already exist — which is why an illustration fails on a missing date of birth or a model with no target return rather than asking for them.

What changed on 9 August

Four changes, approved and shipped: the annuity section is gone, its basis is now a house constant in the API, Weekly is back as a contribution frequency, and capped drawdown is off the picker. Each is recorded with the evidence behind it, because the reasoning is what makes any of them reversible on purpose rather than by accident.

Shipped · 09 Aug 2026

The annuity section is gone; the API sends the house basis

Six questions came off the form. They asked how a fund would be converted to income at retirement — a house assumption, identical for every client of a firm, so a per-client form was the wrong place for it from the start.

The group is still sent. house_annuity_basis() supplies it for any caller that offers none, and that constant lives in routers/illustrations.py rather than in the portal on purpose: a portal that carries the constants is a portal that can forget them, and forgetting means the group is omitted — which files a KFI whose projected-income column reads £0 on an accumulation quote.

Omitting it is still possible, but now has to be asked for: include_annuity_basis: false. Those two intentions — "no preference" and "leave it out" — used to be the same request, which is exactly how the column could go to zero without anyone choosing it.

Shipped · 09 Aug 2026 · states which way it leans

The house basis: level, single life, monthly in advance

Element Spec allows Now sends Which way it biases the figure
annuity_basis M for a pension The group itself Always —
annuity_frequency O Monthly · Quarterly · Half Yearly · Annually Monthly How a pension is actually paid. Neutral.
annuity_timing O Advance · Arrears Advance Market convention. Slightly lower than arrears.
escalation O Fixed · Level · LPI · RPI Level Flatters. Level gives the highest starting income; RPI or LPI would show a lower, inflation-protected one.
percent O Only with Fixed escalation Not sent Meaningless without Fixed.
guaranteed_period O Years Not sent No guarantee is the standard basis. A guarantee lowers the income.
spouse_percentage O Percentage Not sent Flatters. A 50% spouse's pension shows materially less, and most members with a partner would buy one.

This is the conventional illustration basis and the highest income any basis will show. That is stated in the code as well as here, so nobody has to rediscover it. If the firm would rather the projection erred low, the single change is HOUSE_ANNUITY_ESCALATION = "RPI".

Still the right end state: per-firm configuration. Nothing has to move for it — a caller that supplies a basis already overrides the house one wholesale, so a firm setting is a matter of passing one. There is no illustration-assumptions store today, which is the only reason it is not done.

Shipped · 09 Aug 2026

Weekly contributions in, capped drawdown out

Weekly is back as a contribution frequency — the spec has always allowed it (CTC1 §d) and rare is not the same as never.

Capped drawdown is off the picker. It has been closed to new members since April 2015, and offering it produced a request the engine cannot compute properly: capped needs current_max_income, next_review_date and inc_already_taken, and we send none of the three. A picker option that silently mis-projects is worse than an absent one.

The trap those two nearly sprang. One FREQUENCIES constant fed both the contribution and the withdrawal picker — but the spec has two different sets, and a withdrawal may not be Weekly (§f allows Single, Monthly, Quarterly, Half Yearly, Annually, plus 2 Months and 4 Months). Adding Weekly to the shared list would have quietly offered it as a withdrawal frequency too. They are now two constants, and a test pins that Weekly appears in one picker and not the other.

Still open · recommend: pin the vocabulary

The quotation type is settled — one guard is not

Flexi Access Drawdown is a valid value and is what we send. The spec's page-10 table lists only three values, but its own Example 1 uses this one for a Self Invested Personal Pension — our exact product — data-mapping.md records all four, and a test pins that the builder emits it. The table is the incomplete artefact.

What is left: quotation_type is the one closed vocabulary the builder does not put through _one_of. Since CTC answers a bad value with HTTP 200 and an HTML page, a typo introduced later would reach the engine and come back looking like a successful run. Add the four values as a checked vocabulary alongside the others.

Why Unknown is not sent

The spec offers it, and we deliberately leave the element out instead: both fields are optional, so absence says "not recorded", where Unknown is a claim about the client. Same rule for an unmapped gender — Dynamics accepts only Male and Female, so anything else is refused at the boundary rather than coerced.

The Capped drawdown trap

Those last three are capped drawdown only and we never send them — which is why the picker no longer offers Capped (09 Aug 2026). It offers Flexible and UFPLS. Re-adding Capped means adding these three fields at the same time.

Two rules the builder enforces so the engine never has to

Only Transfer Value and Existing Fund Value may be crystallised, and both must be Single. A Member stream without net_gross is rejected here rather than sent incomplete — that field is mandatory for member contributions and was once being left out entirely.

Weekly through Annually are offered on a regular contribution. Single is not: a one-off into the plan is the Initial payment stream. The withdrawal picker is a different list — the spec does not allow a weekly withdrawal, and it does allow a single withdrawal.

One fund, not one per holding — deliberately

A model's allocations name our instruments (ISINs, Seccl asset ids); fund_ref names a fund in CTC's catalogue, and no mapping between the two exists. Projecting the model as a single generic fund at its target return is the honest version of what we can say — and CTC prints fund_name on the document, so the client sees the model's name rather than "Cash".

Note the spec calls rate_of_return the mid rate. On an accumulation quote the printed rates were the COBS standard 2/5/8% regardless, so whether a supplied mid rate displaces the standard one is still unmeasured — the same open question as the model charge.

The group that decides the layout

Omitting withdrawals loses the entire drawdown summary — annual withdrawn, exhaustion age, pre-retirement funds, term length, the year-5 and year-10 values and the whole reduction-in-yield block. quotation_type alone does not decide the document; the presence of withdrawals does. Which is a second reason decision 1 matters less than it looks, and a reason to test both together.

Three things about fees worth knowing before anyone "fixes" them

Under nodb=true there is no standing-data fallback. The spec warns that omitting product_fees makes the engine use the provider's standard charges — with no database attached it charges zero instead. So in our mode omission means "no product fees", not "default fees". If we ever move off nodb=true that reverses, and a partial fee list becomes dangerous.

The spec's adviser-fee frequency list says "Yearly" where every other list in the document says "Annually". We never hit it — the form does not offer a frequency for adviser fees — but anything that later lets an adviser pick one has to send the spelling this table uses, not the one from plan_stream.

The initial charge is a one-off pound amount. Per Annum tells the engine the figure is an annual rate, and binding it to a monthly contribution stream is what made the PDF print Initial adviser charge as monthly. It is Per Frequency, Single, In Advance, and it names a Single stream: the initial payment, else the transfer, else the existing fund. The ongoing percentage stays Monthly, In Arrears, Per Annum, and is not stream-bound.

annuity_basis — now a house constant

The full table is in the shipped-changes section above, with the bias of each choice stated. In short: the group is always sent, on a level single-life monthly-in-advance basis, and include_annuity_basis: false is the only way to omit it.

The element name, settled by measurement rather than by the spec

The spec's table calls the node <annuity_basis>; both of its own examples show <annuity>. We emit <annuity_basis> — and the measured income column proves it is the one the engine reads, because sending it is what filled the column in. Worth recording, since CTC answers a rejected element with HTTP 200 and would not have told us.

Still open, worst first

Everything below is a live discrepancy between what the spec offers, what the code sends, and what a client would see on the document. Ranked by what it does to a KFI rather than by how hard it is to fix — the first two change figures a client reads.

Issue What the client sees Fix
1 product_fees is never sent The scheme's own charges are missing from every illustration. Under nodb=true there is no standing-data fallback — the spec says omission falls back to the provider's standard charges, but with no database attached the engine charges zero. So the projection is net of adviser fees only, and understates the true cost. Send the scheme charges. Note the group is all-or-nothing: a partial list replaces the lot.
2 The crystallisation group is never sent No tax-free-cash figure on a drawdown quote. TFC was inferred from crystallised="Yes" on a stream instead, which is a different thing — and is why the figure came back missing on the drawdown_flexible run. Send the group; it is a separate one from withdrawals.
3 Two growth inputs, neither measured Unknown. The model's management_charge is newly sent and nothing has confirmed CTC applies it; separately, an accumulation quote printed the COBS standard 2/5/8% regardless of our rate_of_return. The spec calls that field the mid rate, so whether ours displaces the standard middle rate is undecided. One calibration run each, with and without, on the same scenario.
4 plus_vat is never set on an adviser fee Adviser charges are projected without VAT. For a firm whose ongoing charge is VATable, the cost is understated by a fifth. Ask on the form, or take it from the fee package — the catalogue already models vatable.
5 quotation_type is unvalidated Nothing today. It is the one closed vocabulary not put through _one_of, and CTC answers a bad value with HTTP 200, so a later typo would look like a successful run. Add the four values as a checked vocabulary.
6 Adviser fees say "Yearly", everything else says "Annually" Nothing today — the form does not offer a frequency for adviser fees. It becomes a silent rejection the moment it does. Use the adviser-fee table's spelling, not plan_stream's, if that picker is ever added.
7 withdrawal_start_date is accepted but never asked Income is projected as starting immediately. For a client deferring drawdown, the projection is wrong from year one. One field in the Income section; the API already takes it.
8 Only one investor, always Individual or Company No spouse, dependant or multi-life illustrations, and no SSAS — Trust is never sent even though the spec allows it and the wrapper list includes SSAS. The record holds relationships already; the group repeats.
9 One generic fund, not a real allocation The client sees the model's name rather than its holdings. Honest, but less than the spec allows. Blocked: no mapping from our instruments to CTC's fund catalogue.
10 Flat fees only — no fee_tiers A tiered charge is projected at a single rate. Seccl's register already publishes tiers, so the data exists and the request cannot carry it. The builder needs the tier shape; the spec's is a list of upper bounds.
One follow-up that is already done

illustrations-report.md §8 still lists "validate exit_age against DOB before calling CTC" as the highest priority open item. It is built — xml_builder.Investor._check_exit_age refuses a retirement age that is not in the future, and one above MAX_EXIT_AGE. A drawdown illustration for someone already past that age is still produced: the quote runs to age 99 (projection_exit_age), and the recorded retirement age is left as it is. The report is stale on that line, not the code.
